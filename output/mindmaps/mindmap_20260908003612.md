# Multiple Linear Regression — I
## 3.1 Method of Least Squares
### Term life insurance demand
#### Response: LNFACE
#### Predictors: EDUCATION, NUMHH, LNINCOME
#### 275 households purchasing term life insurance
### Multivariate data summaries
#### Summary statistics and logarithmic transforms
#### Scatterplot matrices summarize variable pairs
#### Correlations measure linear relationships only
### Regression plane
#### \(y=b_0+b_1x_1+\cdots+b_kx_k\)
#### Plane lies in \(k+1\) dimensions
#### Usually cannot pass through all \(n\) observations
### Least squares criterion
#### \(SS=\sum_{i=1}^{n}[y_i-(b_0+\cdots+b_kx_{ik})]^2\)
#### Minimizes squared deviations from fitted values
#### \(\widehat y=b_0+b_1x_1+\cdots+b_kx_k\)
### Matrix notation and solution
#### \(\mathbf y\) is an \(n\times1\) response vector
#### \(\mathbf X\) is an \(n\times(k+1)\) explanatory matrix
#### Normal equations: \(\mathbf X'\mathbf X\mathbf b=\mathbf X'\mathbf y\)
#### \(\mathbf b=(\mathbf X'\mathbf X)^{-1}\mathbf X'\mathbf y\) when invertible
## 3.2 Linear Regression Model and Properties of Estimators
### Regression function
#### \(\mathrm Ey=\beta_0x_0+\beta_1x_1+\cdots+\beta_kx_k\)
#### \(x_0=1\) gives the intercept
#### \(\beta_j\) is the expected change per unit \(x_j\), holding others fixed
### Coefficient interpretation
#### Positive NUMHH coefficient implies greater expected insurance demand
#### Log response changes can be interpreted as approximate percentage changes
#### Log-log coefficient is an elasticity
### Model assumptions
#### F1: \(\mathrm Ey_i=\beta_0+\beta_1x_{i1}+\cdots+\beta_kx_{ik}\)
#### F2: explanatory variables are nonstochastic
#### F3–F4: constant variance \(\sigma^2\) and independent responses
#### F5: responses are normally distributed
### Estimator properties
#### Property 1: \(\mathbf b\) is unbiased for \(\boldsymbol\beta\)
#### Property 2: \(\operatorname{Var}\mathbf b=\sigma^2(\mathbf X'\mathbf X)^{-1}\)
#### Property 3: \(\mathbf b\) is normally distributed under F1–F5
#### Gauss-Markov: least squares is minimum variance among linear unbiased estimators
## 3.3 Estimation and Goodness of Fit
### Residual standard deviation
#### Residual: \(e_i=y_i-\widehat y_i\)
#### \(s^2=\frac{1}{n-(k+1)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_i-\widehat y_i)^2\)
#### \(s=\sqrt{s^2}\) is the residual standard deviation
#### Error degrees of freedom: \(n-(k+1)\)
### Variability decomposition
#### Total SS = Error SS + Regression SS
#### Total variation is divided into unexplained and explained variation
#### Cross-product terms sum to zero
### Coefficient of determination
#### \(R^2=\frac{\text{Regression SS}}{\text{Total SS}}\)
#### \(R^2\) is the proportion of variability explained
#### \(R^2=[r(y,\widehat y)]^2\)
#### \(R\) is the multiple correlation coefficient
### ANOVA and adjusted fit
#### Regression degrees of freedom: \(k\)
#### Error mean square: \(MSE=s^2\)
#### \(R_a^2=1-\frac{s^2}{s_y^2}\)
#### Adding a variable never decreases unadjusted \(R^2\)
### Term life ANOVA example
#### Regression SS 328.47; Error SS 630.43; Total SS 958.90
#### \(R^2=34.3\%\)
#### \(s=1.525\) and \(R_a^2=33.6\%\)
## 3.4 Statistical Inference for a Single Coefficient
### 3.4.1 The \(t\)-test
#### Test \(H_0:\beta_j=0\) against \(H_a:\beta_j\ne0\)
#### \(se(b_j)=s\sqrt{\text{diagonal element of }(\mathbf X'\mathbf X)^{-1}}\)
#### \(t(b_j)=b_j/se(b_j)\)
#### Degrees of freedom: \(n-(k+1)\)
#### Rule of thumb: \(|t|>2\) indicates an important variable
### Alternative tests and \(p\)-values
#### Tests may use \(H_0:\beta_j=d\)
#### Alternatives may be one-sided or two-sided
#### \(p\)-values describe strength of evidence against the null
### 3.4.2 Confidence intervals
#### \(b_j\pm t_{n-(k+1),1-\alpha/2}se(b_j)\)
#### A hypothesized value outside the interval is rejected by the two-sided test
### 3.4.3 Added variable plots
#### Regress response on all variables except \(x_j\) to obtain \(e_1\)
#### Regress \(x_j\) on the other variables to obtain \(e_2\)
#### Plot \(e_1\) versus \(e_2\)
#### Shows the relationship after controlling for other explanatory variables
### Refrigerator price example
#### PRICE modeled by ECOST, RSIZE, FSIZE, SHELVES, FEATURES
#### Model fit: \(s=60.65\), \(R^2=83.8\%\)
#### ECOST has a negative regression coefficient despite positive pairwise correlation
#### FSIZE can explain the positive PRICE–ECOST association
### 3.4.4 Partial correlation
#### Correlation between added-variable residuals \(e_1\) and \(e_2\)
#### Measures association of \(y\) and \(x_j\) controlling for other predictors
#### \(r=\frac{t(b_j)}{\sqrt{t(b_j)^2+n-(k+1)}}\)
#### Large \(t\)-ratios correspond to large partial correlations
## 3.5 Some Special Explanatory Variables
### 3.5.1 Binary variables
#### Categorical variables identify distinct groups
#### Binary variables are indicator or dummy variables
#### SINGLE equals 1 for single respondents and 0 otherwise
#### Binary coefficient compares the alternative level with the base level
#### Letter plots display a categorized third variable
### 3.5.2 Transforming explanatory variables
#### Regression is linear in parameters, not necessarily in explanatory variables
#### Polynomial regression uses \(x,x^2,\ldots,x^p\)
#### Log transforms can represent gently sloping curves
#### Continuous variables can be categorized with binary transforms
### 3.5.3 Interaction terms
#### Create \(x_3=x_1\times x_2\)
#### \(\frac{\partial \mathrm Ey}{\partial x_1}=\beta_1+\beta_3x_2\)
#### Marginal effect of one variable depends on another variable
#### A linear combination such as \(x_1+x_2\) cannot be separately estimated
### Curvilinear and piecewise models
#### Second-order models include squared and cross-product terms
#### Piecewise linear regression uses binary variables at kinks
#### Charitable-contribution example has a kink at \$97,500
#### Stock commission example has a discontinuity at 100 shares
### Insurance company expense application
#### Segal models expenses using outputs, input prices, squares, and branch interactions
#### Branch indicator \(D\) changes output effects
#### Logarithmic coefficients are interpreted as elasticities
