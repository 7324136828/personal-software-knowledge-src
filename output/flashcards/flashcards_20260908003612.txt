# Multiple Linear Regression I
# Recall deck covering least squares, model assumptions, goodness of fit, inference, and special explanatory variables from Chapter 3. This is part one of a two-part series.
What is the general multiple linear regression fitted plane?	\(\widehat{y}=b_0+b_1x_1+\cdots+b_kx_k\).
What quantity does the method of least squares minimize in multiple regression?	\(SS(b_0^*,\ldots,b_k^*)=\sum_{i=1}^n[y_i-(b_0^*+b_1^*x_{i1}+\cdots+b_k^*x_{ik})]^2\).
In matrix notation, what are the dimensions of \(\mathbf{y}\) and \(\mathbf{X}\)?	\(\mathbf{y}\) is an \(n\times1\) response vector, and \(\mathbf{X}\) is an \(n\times(k+1)\) explanatory-variable matrix.
What role does \(x_{i0}\) usually play in the design matrix?	It is associated with the intercept and is usually identically equal to \(1\).
What are the normal equations for least squares regression?	\(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X}\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{y}\).
Under what condition does the unique least squares solution \(\mathbf{b}=(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{y}\) exist?	When the explanatory variables are not linear combinations of one another, so \(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X}\) is invertible.
In the term life regression of LNFACE on EDUCATION, NUMHH, and LNINCOME, what fitted plane was obtained?	\(\widehat{y}=2.584+0.206x_1+0.306x_2+0.494x_3\), where \(x_1=\) EDUCATION, \(x_2=\) NUMHH, and \(x_3=\) LNINCOME.
How is \(\beta_j\) interpreted for a continuous explanatory variable?	It is the expected change in \(y\) per unit change in \(x_j\), holding all other explanatory variables fixed.
What does the intercept \(\beta_0\) represent in the usual model with \(x_0=1\)?	The expected value of \(y\) when all explanatory variables equal zero; it sets the height of the fitted regression plane.
In a log-response model, what does a small change in \(\ln y\) approximate?	A small proportional change in \(y\), since \(\partial\ln y/\partial x=(\partial y/\partial x)/y\).
What is the economic interpretation of \(\partial\ln y/\partial\ln x\)?	It is an elasticity: the ratio of the percentage change in \(y\) to the percentage change in \(x\).
______ is the coefficient interpretation for a binary variable: the expected increase in \(y\) when moving from the base level \(0\) to the alternative level \(1\).	The binary-variable coefficient.
What is the observables representation assumption F1?	\(\mathrm{E}\,y_i=\beta_0+\beta_1x_{i1}+\cdots+\beta_kx_{ik}\).
What is the error representation assumption E1?	\(y_i=\beta_0+\beta_1x_{i1}+\cdots+\beta_kx_{ik}+\varepsilon_i\).
What do assumptions F2 and E2 state about the explanatory variables?	\(\{x_{i1},\ldots,x_{ik}\}\) are nonstochastic variables.
What do assumptions F3 and F4 state?	\(\mathrm{Var}\,y_i=\sigma^2\), and \(\{y_i\}\) are independent random variables.
What additional assumption is required for normally distributed least squares estimators?	Assumption F5: \(\{y_i\}\) are normally distributed.
Under assumptions F1--F4, what is the expectation of the least squares estimator \(\mathbf{b}\)?	\(\mathrm{E}\,\mathbf{b}=\boldsymbol{\beta}\); \(\mathbf{b}\) is unbiased.
Under assumptions F1--F4, what is \(\mathrm{Var}\,\mathbf{b}\)?	\(\mathrm{Var}\,\mathbf{b}=\sigma^2(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\).
Under assumptions F1--F5, what is the distribution of \(\mathbf{b}\)?	The least squares estimator \(\mathbf{b}\) is normally distributed.
What does the Gauss-Markov theorem say about least squares estimators?	Among estimators that are linear functions of the responses, least squares is the minimum-variance unbiased estimator of \(\boldsymbol{\beta}\).
What is a residual?	The estimated error \(e_i=y_i-\widehat{y}_i\).
What is the mean square error estimator \(s^2\) of \(\sigma^2\)?	\(s^2=\dfrac{1}{n-(k+1)}\sum_{i=1}^n(y_i-\widehat{y}_i)^2\).
What is the residual standard deviation?	\(s=\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{n-(k+1)}\sum_{i=1}^ne_i^2}\); it is also called the standard error of the estimate.
Why does the MSE denominator use \(n-(k+1)\) rather than \(n\)?	Estimating \(k+1\) regression parameters creates dependencies among residuals, leaving only \(n-(k+1)\) unrestricted residuals.
What is the total sum of squares?	\(\mathrm{Total}\ SS=\sum_{i=1}^n(y_i-\bar y)^2\).
State the regression sum-of-squares decomposition.	\(\mathrm{Total}\ SS=\mathrm{Error}\ SS+\mathrm{Regression}\ SS\).
