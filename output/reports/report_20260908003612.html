Multiple Linear Regression I: Estimation, Inference, Fit, and Model Extensions

Executive Summary

This chapter extends linear regression to settings with several explanatory variables, framing the fitted relationship as a regression plane that is linear in its parameters. It develops least squares estimation in scalar and matrix notation, establishes key estimator properties under stated sampling assumptions, and introduces residual standard deviation, \(R^2\), adjusted \(R^2\), ANOVA, coefficient \(t\)-tests, and confidence intervals. Using term life insurance, refrigerator price, life expectancy, and insurance-company expense examples, it emphasizes that multiple regression estimates conditional relationships rather than merely pairwise associations. The chapter also shows how binary indicators, transformations, polynomial terms, interactions, and piecewise-linear specifications broaden the family of relationships that can be represented while retaining a linear regression framework.

From Bivariate Relationships to a Regression Plane

Multiple linear regression considers a response variable together with \(k\) explanatory variables in a sample of size \(n\). The \(i\)th row of the data consists of \((x_{i1}, x_{i2}, \ldots, x_{ik}, y_i)\), so each entity contributes \(k+1\) measurements. The central geometric representation is a plane in \(k+1\) dimensions, written as \(y=b_0+b_1x_1+\cdots+b_kx_k\). Because typical datasets contain more observations than the \(k+1\) needed to determine a plane, a single plane generally cannot pass through every observed point.\n\nThe term life insurance example motivates the move to several predictors. For 275 households that purchased term life insurance, the response is logarithmic face amount, LNFACE, and the explanatory variables are years of EDUCATION, household size NUMHH, and logarithmic income LNINCOME. Summary statistics and scatterplots indicate that FACE and INCOME are right-skewed, while their logarithmic transforms exhibit a relationship more readily approximated by a line. Scatterplot and correlation matrices are useful preliminary tools, but each describes only pairwise relationships and cannot quantify relationships among several variables jointly.

In the general multiple-regression setting, the data consist of \(k\) explanatory variables and one response variable observed for a sample of size \(n\).

Least Squares Estimation and Matrix Formulation

The least squares method chooses coefficient values that minimize the sum of squared vertical deviations between observed and fitted responses: \(SS(b_0^*,\ldots,b_k^*)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}[y_i-(b_0^*+b_1^*x_{i1}+\cdots+b_k^*x_{ik})]^2\). Once estimated, the fitted regression plane is \(\widehat y=b_0+b_1x_1+\cdots+b_kx_k\). In the term life application, fitting LNFACE using EDUCATION, NUMHH, and LNINCOME produces \(\widehat y=2.584+0.206x_1+0.306x_2+0.494x_3\).\n\nMatrix notation expresses the response values as an \(n\times1\) vector \(\mathbf y\), the explanatory variables as an \(n\times(k+1)\) matrix \(\mathbf X\), and the coefficients as \(\mathbf b=(b_0,b_1,\ldots,b_k)'\). The least squares solution satisfies the normal equations \(\mathbf X'\mathbf X\mathbf b=\mathbf X'\mathbf y\). If the explanatory variables are not linear combinations of one another, \(\mathbf X'\mathbf X\) is invertible and the unique least squares estimator is \(\mathbf b=(\mathbf X'\mathbf X)^{-1}\mathbf X'\mathbf y\).

Least squares estimates minimize the sum of squared deviations between observed responses and the plane defined by the candidate coefficients.

Regression Function, Interpretation, and Assumptions

The multiple linear regression function is \(\mathrm{E}y=\beta_0x_0+\beta_1x_1+\cdots+\beta_kx_k\), ordinarily with \(x_0=1\) for the intercept. The intercept is the expected value of the response when every explanatory variable equals zero, whereas, for a continuously varying predictor, \(\beta_j\) is interpreted as the expected change in \(y\) per unit change in \(x_j\), holding all other explanatory variables fixed. In the term life model, the positive coefficients on EDUCATION, NUMHH, and LNINCOME support conditional interpretations of higher fitted log face amount as each predictor increases while the others are held fixed.\n\nThe chapter provides equivalent observables and error representations of the sampling assumptions. In the observables representation, expected responses are linear in the explanatory variables, explanatory variables are nonstochastic, responses have common variance \(\sigma^2\), responses are independent, and responses are normally distributed. In the error representation, the response equals its regression function plus an additive error, with errors having mean zero, common variance, independence, and normality. The stated sampling motivation treats each unique explanatory-variable combination as a stratum from which responses are sampled.

The regression function is linear in the parameters and is written as \(\mathrm{E}y=\beta_0x_0+\beta_1x_1+\cdots+\beta_kx_k\), with \(x_0=1\) used for the intercept in the chapter's default formulation.

Estimator Properties and Goodness of Fit

Under assumptions F1–F4, the least squares estimator is unbiased for the parameter vector and has variance \(\operatorname{Var}(\mathbf b)=\sigma^2(\mathbf X'\mathbf X)^{-1}\). The Gauss-Markov theorem further identifies least squares as the minimum-variance unbiased estimator within the class of estimators that are linear functions of the responses. Adding normality, assumption F5, implies that the least squares coefficient vector is normally distributed; this result supports the chapter's later inference procedures.\n\nFor variability around the fitted plane, the mean square error is \(s^2=[n-(k+1)]^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_i-\widehat y_i)^2\), and \(s=\sqrt{s^2}\) is the residual standard deviation. The total sum of squares decomposes into error and regression sums of squares, yielding \(R^2=\mathrm{Regression\ SS}/\mathrm{Total\ SS}\), interpreted as the proportion of variability explained by the regression function. Because ordinary \(R^2\) cannot decrease when a predictor is added, the adjusted measure \(R_a^2=1-s^2/s_y^2\) accounts for degrees of freedom. For the term life model, the reported ANOVA quantities imply \(R^2=34.3\%\), \(s=1.525\), and adjusted \(R_a^2=33.6\%\).

Under assumptions F1–F4, \(\mathbf b\) is an unbiased estimator of \(\boldsymbol\beta\).

Single-Coefficient Inference and Conditional Relationships

To assess whether an individual explanatory variable is important, the chapter tests \(H_0:\beta_j=0\) against an alternative such as \(H_a:\beta_j\ne0\). The standard error of \(b_j\) is obtained from the residual standard deviation and the relevant diagonal element of \((\mathbf X'\mathbf X)^{-1}\), and the test statistic is \(t(b_j)=b_j/se(b_j)\). Under the model assumptions and the null hypothesis, this statistic follows a \(t\) distribution with \(n-(k+1)\) degrees of freedom. A two-sided test rejects when the absolute \(t\)-ratio exceeds the relevant critical value; for large samples, the chapter gives two in absolute value as a practical rule of thumb for identifying an important variable.\n\nThe refrigerator example distinguishes pairwise correlation from a conditional regression relationship. PRICE and ECOST have a positive raw correlation, but the fitted multiple regression gives ECOST a negative coefficient of \(-6.96\), conditional on refrigerator and freezer size, shelves, and features. An added variable plot displays residuals from PRICE regressed on the other predictors against residuals from ECOST regressed on those same predictors; it portrays their relationship after controlling for the remaining explanatory variables. Its correlation is the partial correlation coefficient, which can also be computed from the coefficient \(t\)-ratio as \(r(y,x_j\mid\text{others})=t(b_j)/\sqrt{t(b_j)^2+n-(k+1)}\).

The coefficient \(t\)-ratio is \(t(b_j)=b_j/se(b_j)\), and under the null hypothesis it has a \(t\) distribution with \(n-(k+1)\) degrees of freedom when assumptions F1–F5 hold.

Binary Variables, Transformations, Interactions, and Piecewise Models

The linear regression framework is flexible because linearity refers to the parameters, not necessarily to the functional form of the explanatory variables. A binary variable is coded to indicate membership in one of two categories. In the term life example, SINGLE equals one for a single respondent and zero otherwise. In a model with LNINCOME and SINGLE, the binary coefficient gives the expected difference in the response between the alternative category and the base category, holding the continuous predictor fixed. The text also labels binary variables as indicator variables or dummy variables.\n\nTransformations permit nonlinear response functions while preserving linearity in coefficients. Polynomial regression is obtained by treating \(x, x^2,\ldots,x^p\) as separate explanatory variables, while \(\ln x\) offers another transformed predictor. Interactions such as \(x_1x_2\) allow the marginal association of one predictor to depend on another: for \(\mathrm{E}y=\beta_0+\beta_1x_1+\beta_2x_2+\beta_3x_1x_2\), the partial derivative with respect to \(x_1\) is \(\beta_1+\beta_3x_2\). Binary variables can also create piecewise linear models with kinks or discontinuities, allowing different fitted slopes or intercepts above and below specified thresholds.

Conclusions

The chapter establishes multiple linear regression as a method for estimating and interpreting conditional relationships among a response and several explanatory variables. Least squares supplies a fitted plane and, under the stated assumptions, yields unbiased estimators with a known variance form and normal sampling distribution when normality holds. Residual standard deviation, ANOVA, \(R^2\), and adjusted \(R^2\) assess fit, while \(t\)-tests, confidence intervals, added variable plots, and partial correlations support variable-specific inference and interpretation. Most importantly, the framework remains linear in coefficients while accommodating transformed variables, categorical indicators, interactions, and piecewise specifications, enabling substantially richer response functions than an additive straight-line model in the original predictors.
