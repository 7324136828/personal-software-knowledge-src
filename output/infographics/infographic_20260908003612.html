Matrix solution and estimator properties

Normal equations vs solution: \(\mathbf X'\mathbf X\mathbf b=\mathbf X'\mathbf y\) vs \(\mathbf b=(\mathbf X'\mathbf X)^{-1}\mathbf X'\mathbf y\)

Unbiasedness vs precision: \(\mathrm E\mathbf b=\boldsymbol\beta\) vs \(\mathrm{Var}\mathbf b=\sigma^2(\mathbf X'\mathbf X)^{-1}\)

F1–F4 vs F1–F5: Gauss–Markov gives minimum-variance unbiased linear estimation vs normality additionally gives a normal distribution for \(\mathbf b\)

Test whether one coefficient matters

State \(H_0:\beta_j=0\) against \(H_a:\beta_j\ne0\).

Compute \(se(b_j)\) from \(s\) and the corresponding diagonal element of \((\mathbf X'\mathbf X)^{-1}\).

Form \(t(b_j)=b_j/se(b_j)\) with \(df=n-(k+1)\).

Reject \(H_0\) when \u007c\(t(b_j)\)\u007c exceeds the appropriate \(t\)-critical value; as a rule of thumb at 5%, a magnitude above two indicates importance.

Extending the linear model

Binary variable vs continuous variable: a binary coefficient compares the alternative level with its base level; a continuous coefficient is an expected change per unit change, holding other variables fixed.

Transformation vs nonlinear regression function: \(x, x^2,\ldots,x^p\) or \(\ln x\) can be explanatory variables while the model remains linear in its parameters.

Interaction vs additive effects: with \(x_3=x_1x_2\), \(\partial\mathrm E y/\partial x_1=\beta_1+\beta_3x_2\), so the effect of one variable depends on the other.

Pairwise correlation vs partial correlation: an added variable plot controls for other explanatory variables and can reveal a relationship different from the unadjusted correlation.
