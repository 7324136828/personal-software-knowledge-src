# Multiple Linear Regression — I

*A visual guide to least squares, model fit, inference, and special explanatory variables*

## Panel 1: 1. Least Squares Foundations

### From multivariate data to a fitted plane
1. Collect \(n\) observations with \(k\) explanatory variables and one response: \((x_{i1},\ldots,x_{ik},y_i)\).
1. Inspect variables individually; use transformations such as LNFACE and LNINCOME when skewness and nonlinear patterns suggest them.
1. Use scatterplot matrices and correlation matrices to summarize pairwise relationships.
1. Fit the regression plane \(\widehat y=b_0+b_1x_1+\cdots+b_kx_k\).

### Least squares objective
> Determine the coefficients that minimize the sum of squared differences between observed and fitted responses.
> — Equation (3.1)

## Panel 2: 2. Model Fit and Inference

### Matrix solution and estimator properties
1. Normal equations vs solution: \(\mathbf X'\mathbf X\mathbf b=\mathbf X'\mathbf y\) vs \(\mathbf b=(\mathbf X'\mathbf X)^{-1}\mathbf X'\mathbf y\)
1. Unbiasedness vs precision: \(\mathrm E\mathbf b=\boldsymbol\beta\) vs \(\mathrm{Var}\mathbf b=\sigma^2(\mathbf X'\mathbf X)^{-1}\)
1. F1–F4 vs F1–F5: Gauss–Markov gives minimum-variance unbiased linear estimation vs normality additionally gives a normal distribution for \(\mathbf b\)

### Term life ANOVA: variation in LNFACE

### Goodness-of-fit measures
**\(R^2=34.3\%\quad s=1.525\quad R_a^2=33.6\%\)**

  \(R^2\) is the proportion of variability explained; \(s\) is the residual standard deviation

### Test whether one coefficient matters
1. State \(H_0:\beta_j=0\) against \(H_a:\beta_j\ne0\).
1. Compute \(se(b_j)\) from \(s\) and the corresponding diagonal element of \((\mathbf X'\mathbf X)^{-1}\).
1. Form \(t(b_j)=b_j/se(b_j)\) with \(df=n-(k+1)\).
1. Reject \(H_0\) when \u007c\(t(b_j)\)\u007c exceeds the appropriate \(t\)-critical value; as a rule of thumb at 5%, a magnitude above two indicates importance.

## Panel 3: 3. Special Variables and Interpretation

### Extending the linear model
1. Binary variable vs continuous variable: a binary coefficient compares the alternative level with its base level; a continuous coefficient is an expected change per unit change, holding other variables fixed.
1. Transformation vs nonlinear regression function: \(x, x^2,\ldots,x^p\) or \(\ln x\) can be explanatory variables while the model remains linear in its parameters.
1. Interaction vs additive effects: with \(x_3=x_1x_2\), \(\partial\mathrm E y/\partial x_1=\beta_1+\beta_3x_2\), so the effect of one variable depends on the other.
1. Pairwise correlation vs partial correlation: an added variable plot controls for other explanatory variables and can reveal a relationship different from the unadjusted correlation.

### Partial correlation from a t-ratio
**\(r(y,x_j\mid\text{other }x)=\dfrac{t(b_j)}{\sqrt{t(b_j)^2+n-(k+1)}}\)**

  Equation (3.11): correlation after controlling for the other explanatory variables
